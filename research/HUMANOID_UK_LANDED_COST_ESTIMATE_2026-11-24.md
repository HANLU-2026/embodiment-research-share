## 2. Customs and VAT — do not present an unapproved 0% duty as a fact

- [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-shopping/vat-duties): UK VAT standard **20%**. [HMRC import VAT valuation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/working-out-the-vat-value-using-the-customs-value-of-the-imported-goods) includes import duties and expenses such as packing, freight and insurance up to the UK destination as applicable. Actual import value/exchange rate determined on customs paperwork.
- **Classification**: HS **8479500000** covers *industrial robots, not elsewhere specified*. Some third-party tariff trackers indicate **0% UK import duty under this heading**, but this is **NOT confirmation a private-household humanoid belongs in that code** (or a binding rate/origin decision). Verify via [UK Trade Tariff](https://www.gov.uk/trade-tariff), importer/broker and if needed an [HMRC Advance Tariff Ruling](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-what-youll-need-to-get-a-legally-binding-decision-on-a-commodity-code).
- **Planning sensitivity, NOT an applicable forecast tariff:** show two scenarios **0% duty** versus **5% duty** on assumed customs basis; 5% is an arbitrary adverse scenario, not a claimed statutory rate. A different binding classification may produce a result outside the scenario.
- EU *seller* location is not evidence of EU **preferential originating** status. Imported robots sold from Germany or Netherlands may still be of Chinese origin. No automatic UK-EU zero duty.
- For foreign supplier model: `C = hardware GBP + modelled freight/DG/clearance/cargo insurance to UK destination`. Estimated cash before household insurance = `(C × (1 + duty)) × 1.20`; simplified sensitivity, with any separate UK services handled according to invoice and VAT rules.
- For verified UK seller ex-VAT model: `(hardware GBP + modelled UK delivery/commissioning) × 1.20`. Do not append foreign-duty/import-VAT charges to the end purchaser unless explicitly unpaid under supply contract.
- If a UK private homeowner buys the machine, **assume VAT is a real cost not recoverable input VAT**; don't implicitly borrow a UK business VAT registration or tax relief. DDP/Incoterms, VAT already paid or a UK VAT-registered buyer alter the accounting.

## 3. Lithium batteries are a feasibility/cost gate, not an invented fixed surcharge

All candidates' batteries are well above 100Wh where OEM battery specs published (X2 ~500Wh, GR-3 702+234Wh, NEO 842Wh; Gen4 exact Wh unknown). Battery type, packing and mode determine carrier rules: **UN3480** (batteries alone) vs **UN3481** (packed with or inside equipment), with route-specific restrictions; do not assume all batteries must be removed or all air freight impossible.

[2026 IATA lithium-battery guidance](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/dangerous-goods/lithium-batteries/) distinguishes **PI 966** (packed with equipment) from **PI 967** (contained in equipment): <=30% SoC requirement applies to PI966 air shipments subject to defined exceptions/approvals, whereas 30% for PI967 is generally a strong recommendation, not universal mandate. Road and sea use other regimes: [UK official multimodal rules](https://www.gov.uk/moving-dangerous-goods/rules-for-different-types-of-transport).

Ask actual carrier/OEM for packed crate size/chargeable weight, battery test summary UN38.3, transport classification, IMDG/ADR/IATA route, dangerous-goods acceptance, pack/SoC, insurance, customs brokerage, last-mile UK residential delivery, powered or unpowered unloading, delivery/recovery and later **defective-battery return**. The freight allowances below are **finance-model inputs**, not carrier offers or universal DG surcharges.
